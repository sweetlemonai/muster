Muster · where it stands, October 2026

A task desk for a crew of AI coding agents

You say what you want in one line. A crew of AI agents shapes it into a task, builds it, checks it and hands it back. You only see what really needs you.

The problem

AI coding agents can now build real features on their own, and one person can have several working at once. The hard part is no longer the code. It's running the crew: what each agent is doing, what it's stuck on, whether "done" really means done, and which questions truly need you.

With a chat window per agent, all of that lives in your head. You lose track of which window had which job, answer the same question twice, two agents start the same work, and "finished" means whatever the agent said.

Muster is where that work lives instead: every task, who's on it, what stage it's in, what it waits for, what it cost, and what needs you. The agents and you read and change the same list, under the same rules.

Why not just use Jira, Linear or Trello?

Those boards assume people. Agents behave differently, so the board has to as well.

Nothing skips a step. The builder never tests its own work, and the tool, not the agent, moves the card.

What you see

Waiting for you

One list of everything that needs you, across every project: questions, things to try, permissions. The Dock badge and a Mac notification count the same list.

Your check

Each finished task says in plain words what was built, what Accept does and how each requirement was proven, with screenshots. Accept, or send it back with a reason.

Sessions

Every agent session on the Mac: which need input, which are working, which are done, and the story of each one's work.

Many projects

One sidebar for every project, showing which are busy, which are stalled and which need you, grouped and coloured your way.

Designed for a busy, distractible mind

Muster is built for an owner with ADHD, where out of sight means out of mind. So:

  • Everything visible, nothing hidden. No pop-ups or overlays: every state and action is on screen but out of the way.
  • Ask only what's mine. Agents batch their questions, and each question explains what it is, the options, and what they'd pick.
  • One place per job. Three display modes (ADHD, Standard, Focus) show more or less at once.
  • No babysitting. The work moves without you. You come in for decisions and for trying the result.

Trust: nothing lands on a promise

1 · It builds

Every test and check passes on the exact code that will land.

2 · It's reviewed

A different agent reviews the change and records its findings. The builder fixes them or argues back.

3 · It does what was asked

Each requirement is ticked with its proof: a test, a measurement or a screenshot. Without all three, the merge refuses.

Private, and yours

  • Runs on your Mac. Tasks are plain files in your own project, with their own history.
  • No analytics, no crash reports, nothing sent anywhere except to the AI model doing the work.
  • Uses your own AI subscriptions and never handles your logins.

Honest challenges

  • Cost. Careful reviews are what make the work trustworthy, and they're the biggest cost. Finding the right level of checking per task is open.
  • Parallel work collides. Agents working side by side touch the same files, and the merges cost time and tokens.
  • The owner is still the bottleneck for trying finished work. Visual gates are meant to fix that.
  • Mac only, one owner. No teams, no web version, no other operating systems yet.
